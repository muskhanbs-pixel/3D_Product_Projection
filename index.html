02 — Explainer

Two cameras.
Two rules.

Both diagrams show two identical objects from the side. One stays near, the other keeps moving away. Watch what lands on the screen.

Perspective

Farther means smaller.

Every ray travels to a single point: the eye. The farther an object is, the steeper its rays, and the smaller it lands on the screen.

100% Far object's size on screen,
compared to the near one
Projection y′ = f · y / z
Orthographic

Distance doesn't matter.

Rays travel in parallel, straight onto the screen. Moving an object farther away changes nothing about its size.

100% Far object's size on screen,
compared to the near one
Projection y′ = y

04 — Compare

Side by side,
point by point.

Everything you just saw in the lab, summarized as a spec sheet.

Property Perspective Orthographic
Depth perception Strong Size and converging lines instantly show what's near or far. Weak Depth can only be read from overlap and position.
Object size Changes with distance Farther objects appear smaller: y′ = f · y / z. Always true to scale Size never depends on distance: y′ = y.
Realism High Matches how human eyes and real cameras see. Low Looks technical and diagram-like, by design.
Field of view An angle (FOV°) The view volume is a pyramid that widens with distance. A fixed width The view volume is a box. Zoom scales it instead.
Parallel lines Converge They meet at vanishing points, like railway tracks. Stay parallel They never meet, no matter how far they go.
Measurement Unreliable On-screen lengths depend on distance, so you can't measure from the image. Accurate Equal lengths look equal, so you can measure straight from the view.
Camera class THREE.PerspectiveCamera(fov, aspect, near, far) THREE.OrthographicCamera(left, right, top, bottom, near, far)
Common uses Experiences Games, VR, AR, film, simulations, product visualization. Precision work CAD, engineering, architecture, technical drawings, manufacturing.

05 — In the real world

Where each
projection lives.

Perspective makes things feel real. Orthographic makes things measurable. Most of the 3D you see every day uses one or the other on purpose.

Feels real Measurable
Perspective

Games

First and third-person games use perspective so worlds feel deep and immersive. Every road, corridor and horizon depends on things shrinking with distance.

Virtual reality

Two perspective cameras, one per eye, create true 3D depth.

Augmented reality

Virtual objects must match the phone camera's real perspective.

Film & VFX

CGI must match the real lens, or effects look "pasted on."

Simulations

Flight and driving simulators need realistic depth to train real reflexes.

Perspective

Product visualization

Online 3D configurators and product shots use perspective so products look exactly like they would in your hands. Like the speaker in our lab.

Orthographic

CAD

Engineers design parts in front, top and side views, all orthographic, so every line can be measured exactly.

Engineering

Machine parts must fit to the millimeter. No distortion allowed.

Architecture

Floor plans and elevations show true proportions of every wall.

Technical drawings

Blueprints and manuals rely on views you can scale and trust.

Measurement

Maps and inspection tools measure lengths straight off the image.

Manufacturing

CNC machines and 3D printers follow exact, undistorted dimensions.

Isometric games

Strategy and puzzle games use ortho for that tidy "diorama" look. 🎮
